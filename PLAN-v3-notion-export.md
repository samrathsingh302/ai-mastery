# # PART 1 — THE AI-AGE THESIS (why the plan is shaped like this)
Jobs are restructuring around AI. The Reddit/YouTube sweep confirms the split (their words, not mine):
\*\*Commoditising:\*\* boilerplate coding, CRUD tickets, first-draft anything. \*"The people AI multiplies are the ones who already understand code."\* \*\*Appreciating:\*\* ① verification & judgment (reading code critically, evals, testing, security review — the more code AI writes, the scarcer this gets) ② architecture & problem framing ③ security (AI-accelerated code ships AI-accelerated vulns) ④ orchestration leverage (you, already) ⑤ distribution & trust (your PSOC presidency; AI doesn't shake hands).
\*\*The two lines the community repeats loudest (both are now house law):\*\* 1. \*\*"There is no ChatGPT in the interview."\*\* (652-pt r/learnprogramming thread; hiring managers describe eye-tracked, extension-monitored tests.) Everything below is designed so interview-you and real-you are the same person. 2. \*\*Never let AI write code you couldn't write yourself\*\* (105-pt top comment). AI explains, reviews, quizzes — it doesn't produce code you can't produce. Kernighan's Law is the reason: debugging is twice as hard as writing, so code at the edge of your comprehension is \*undebuggable by you\*.
\*\*Your strategy is a barbell:\*\* maximum AI leverage on one side (you're ahead), genuine unassisted fundamentals on the other (this plan). The middle — "codes with autocomplete" — is where jobs die. Standing rules: - \*\*20% of all coding hours are AI-free\*\* (docs + brain only). Track it in the journal. - \*\*The outage test, weekly:\*\* "if Anthropic went down for a week, what % of my productivity survives?" — score it honestly in /weekly-review; the number must trend up. \*\*Concrete drill (added 08/07/2026):\*\* pick ONE open issue from the audit-stocked lists (hevy-brain #13/#14, cv-editor #1, loop-runner #1–#3 on GitHub; VAULT-1/2 in \`dev/_global/tasks.md\`) and fix it fully AI-free — counts toward the 20% quota, zero setup required. - \*\*Walls are the curriculum.\*\* The 145-pt consensus: competence is built by being stuck until it clicks. Tutorials are skippable; wall-hitting is not. When stuck, you get 30+ minutes of genuine struggle before Claude may even \*hint\*.
# # PART 2 — THE CLAUDE MAX TUTOR PLAYBOOK (your subscription as the teaching engine)
You have Claude Max — used correctly it's a world-class private tutor; used lazily it's the "illusion of knowing" machine the threads warn about (\*"the trick AI plays on you is that it makes you think you could do it"\*). Research note: studies cited in the sweep found AI-as-tutor users outperformed peers while answer-fetchers underperformed even the no-AI group. Same tool; opposite outcomes; the difference is the contract.
\*\*The Tutor Contract (paste at the start of every study session — /study-session does it for you):\*\* \> You are my tutor, not my solver. Rules: (1) NEVER show me a full solution — explain concepts, show a \*different\* example, then make me do mine. (2) Socratic by default: ask me questions before answering mine. (3) When I submit work, grade it harshly, name every flaw, but make me fix it myself. (4) Quiz me at the end: 5 questions, no notes. (5) Draft 3–8 Anki cards from what I got wrong (I curate before import). (6) If I ask you to just do it, refuse and remind me of the interview.
\*\*The eight tutor moves (use daily):\*\* 1. \*\*Teach-me-the-map\*\* (breadth): "give me the 80/20 map of \<topic\>: the 5 concepts that matter, how they connect, what I can ignore for now" — 1 hour per topic, this is the whole BREADTH layer's engine. 2. \*\*Explain-back grading:\*\* you explain, Claude grades harshly against the source. 3. \*\*Close-window-retype\*\* (from the sweep, twice-recommended): read Claude's explanation → close it → retype/rebuild from memory → diff. 4. \*\*Interactive artifacts:\*\* have Claude build you custom quiz apps, visualisers, flashcard games, code-tracing exercises as HTML artifacts on demand — "build me an interactive quiz on Python scoping with 10 questions that get harder" works today and is unreasonably effective. Ask for one per topic. 5. \*\*Weakness-calibrated drills:\*\* paste your journal misses; "generate 5 exercises targeting exactly these gaps." 6. \*\*The bug hunt:\*\* "write me a 30-line function with 3 subtle bugs; I find them" — daily dojo fuel, difficulty auto-scales. \*\*Hunt using the \`debugging-to-root-cause\` loop by hand\*\* (reproduce → read the actual error → one hypothesis at a time → prove the fix against the repro): interview-you practises the exact procedure agent-you runs. 7. \*\*Mock interviews:\*\* cold, no notes, harsh grading (you already prefer harsh). 8. \*\*Anki pipeline:\*\* Claude drafts atomic cards → YOU review/edit → import. (Community consensus: never import unreviewed AI decks.)
\*\*Anti-sycophancy rule:\*\* Claude folds when challenged (\*"it's designed to back down every single time"\*) — so verify against docs/tests/runs, never against its confidence. \*\*When NOT to use Claude:\*\* during struggle reps, during the 20% quota, during the first 30 minutes of any wall.
\*\*The portability angle (added 08/07/2026):\*\* \`dev/_global/OPERATING-ANY-AGENT.md\` doubles as a learning artifact — drop a \*different\* AI (GPT, Gemini, anything) into a small task operating purely from it, then grade the compliance yourself. Operating an unfamiliar model from a written contract is Tier 3's judge-models-with-a-method, live.
