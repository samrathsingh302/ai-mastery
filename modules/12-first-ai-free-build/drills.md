# Drills — module 12

## Anki-importable block (tab-separated: Front ⇥ Back)

What does AI-free actually forbid?	Claude, autocomplete/Copilot, AI search summaries, concept chats, pasting errors in. Docs, your own notes, help(), the REPL and paper are allowed.
The unassisted wall protocol (5 steps)?	Re-read the error bottom-up · reproduce in the smallest snippet · inspect real values · one written hypothesis at a time · 45 min no progress = log it and return tomorrow.
The honesty clause?	Log every breach with what and how long — a quantified breach is data; a hidden one corrupts your baseline forever.
Why grade BEFORE showing anyone?	Grading first forces criteria; grading after invites rationalising whatever you happened to build.
What makes a test worth having?	It would CATCH a real break — prove it by breaking the code on purpose and watching the test fail.
Why must errors exit non-zero?	Exit codes are how other programs (and future you, in scripts) detect failure — a traceback is not an interface.
The exit test, and its pass mark?	A cold 30-minute interview sim: projects + Python + how LLMs work + judgement. 9+/12 to proceed with momentum.
What does a low sim score mean?	The fast track found your gaps — which is its job. Misses become drills and cards; re-sit in a fortnight. The trend is the instrument.
The outage test question?	"If Anthropic vanished for a week, what % of my productivity survives?" — recorded with a date, must trend up.
Why build a tool you'll actually use?	A tool you don't use taught you syntax; a tool you use teaches you design — you meet your own decisions again after three days.
What is the most valuable artefact of this module?	A wall you crossed alone — more valuable than the finished tool.
