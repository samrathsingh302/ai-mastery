# Drills — module 09

## Anki-importable block (tab-separated: Front ⇥ Back)

An eval's three irreducible parts?	Fixed tasks + a scoring method + the discipline not to peek (blindness).
The two scoring families?	Programmatic (a checker decides: exact/tests/regex) and rubric (a human grades against written criteria, blind).
Why must rubric grading be blind?	You WILL favour the model you expect to win — shuffling + anonymising is the only honest fix.
Four properties of a good eval task?	Representative · one skill per task · verifiable · crisply stated (a wrong answer can't blame the prompt).
What is contamination?	The answer exists as memorised text (famous puzzles) — the task measures recall of the internet, not capability.
Why is n=1 an anecdote?	Models are stochastic — one run's single-task differences are noise; patterns across task FAMILIES are the signal.
What does a per-family subtotal reveal that the total hides?	The capability profile — a model can win code and lose writing (jagged intelligence, measured).
"Model X felt smarter" — the engineering translation?	No measurement occurred. Run the harness.
The fixture story worth remembering (9–9 dead heat)?	Equal totals, opposite profiles: A followed output contracts exactly; B explained better but ignored instructions — which one you want depends on the JOB.
What made fixture-B fail four programmatic tasks?	Instruction-following: extra prose around a correct answer still fails an exact-match contract ("reply with ONLY…" means only).
Why "reply with ONLY the number" in task prompts?	The output contract makes programmatic checking possible — eval design and prompt design are the same skill.
What would make your harness lie to you (4 ways)?	Contaminated tasks · leading/ambiguous prompts · unblinded grading · reading n=1 noise as signal.
Criteria vs vibes — the test?	A stranger applying your criteria reaches your score ±0; "is it good?" fails that instantly.
Where does this module scale to in the plan?	Tier 3's 20-task harness → Tier 4's agent-with-evals and RAG-with-evals — same method, bigger surface.
